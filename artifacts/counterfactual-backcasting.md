## 1. Target Discipline Identification

The Ontological Closure program depends on four core disciplines. For each, we identify the current state and a hypothetically advanced "target state" — what these disciplines would look like if OC claims were fully absorbed and operationalized.

| Discipline | Current State (2026) | Target State |
|:-----------|:---------------------|:-------------|
| **Stratification Theory** (measurement hierarchy, scale separation) | Measurement is treated as a black-box collapse or decoherence. No formal theory of measurement AS stratification of information across scales. | Every measurement protocol specifies: (a) the stratum (scale) at which the measurement is valid, (b) the information lost to coarser strata, (c) the fidelity bound derived from the stratum separation. Measurement is *stratified*, not monolithic. |
| **Computability Theory** (computable analysis, constructive mathematics) | Computable analysis is a niche subfield. Physics uses classical ℝ without computability constraints. Bishop-style constructive mathematics is used by logicians, not physicists. | Every physical theory states its computability class explicitly. A "physical prediction" includes a modulus of convergence. PhD physicists are trained in computable analysis as a core competency alongside calculus. |
| **p-adic Physics / Number Theory** (p-adic completions, adelic methods) | p-adic methods in physics are a fringe curiosity. Vladimirov-Volovich program (1980s-90s) produced interesting mathematics but zero confirmed physical predictions. | p-adic completions are STANDARD tools in QFT. Every quantum system is characterized by its p-adic valuation signature alongside its symmetry group. A "p-adic spectrometer" is a real laboratory instrument measuring these signatures. |
| **Valuation Theory** (p-adic valuations, norms, ultrametricity) | Valuation theory is pure number theory. Physicists do not know what a valuation is. The word "norm" in physics means Hilbert-space norm, never a p-adic norm. | Valuation theory is taught in physics methods courses. Ultrametricity is recognized as the natural topology for any hierarchically organized physical system — from energy levels (atomic spectra) to error-correction codes. |

## 2. Tiered Fork Classification

We backcast across four fork tiers, asking: *what would have to be true for this target state to already exist?*

### Tier 1: ~20-Year Forks (2000s Reprioritization → Impacts by 2020s)

A single research program, reprioritized ~20 years ago, produces a different technology stack today. These forks are **theoretically achievable within a single career span** — an alternative funding decision in 2005 produces a different 2025.

### Tier 2: ~60-Year Forks (1960s Reprioritization → Impacts by 2000s)

Coordinated advancement across 2-3 disciplines, begun in the 1960s, produces fundamentally different computing/measurement paradigms by the 2000s. These forks require **institutional coordination across disciplines** — not just one lab, but a field-level realignment.

### Tier 3: ~120-Year Forks (1900s Reprioritization → Impacts by 1980s)

Incompatible mathematical foundations were chosen at the dawn of modern mathematics. Reversing these choices requires **rewriting the foundations of a discipline** — not building on top, but replacing the axioms.

### Tier 4: Alternate Axioms (Indefinite Timeline)

The axioms themselves differ. These are not "forks" in the historical sense — they are counterfactual worlds where mathematics developed along fundamentally different lines. Useful as stress-tests for the OC program's assumptions, not as actionable recommendations.

## 6. Near-Term Fork Recommendations (Actionable Tier 1)

These Tier 1 forks are the most actionable outputs of the backcasting exercise. They become recommendations for the trilogy paper's "Future Work" section.

1. **Textbook Intervention (Cost: ~$0):** Write and self-publish a 20-page "p-Adic Methods for Physicists" primer on arXiv. Target: first-year physics graduate students. If adopted by even 2 instructors, the 2010 textbook fork becomes retroactively real — we cannot change 2010, but we can make 2026 the start.

2. **Benchmark Bounty (Cost: ~$5,000):** Offer a prize for the best open-source implementation of a "p-adic valuation gap" metric for quantum computing benchmarks. This directly creates the Tier 1 fork artifact even without institutional funding.

3. **Lattice QCD Re-Analysis (Cost: Published data, ~$0):** Download published lattice QCD data from the International Lattice Data Grid. Re-analyze existing results with computable convergence modulus methodology. This demonstrates the Tier 1 fork's VALUE without waiting for new experiments — proving that ℝ_c methods would have already improved results if adopted.

4. **Constructive QM Course (Cost: ~$0 for materials):** Create and release a complete "Constructive Quantum Mechanics" course (lecture notes + problem sets) under CC-BY. The Tier 2 fork's barrier was EDUCATION — this removes it, one open-access course at a time.
